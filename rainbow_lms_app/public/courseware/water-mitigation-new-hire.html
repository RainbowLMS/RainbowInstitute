Course Purpose

This interactive course introduces new restoration employees to the basic workflow of a professional water mitigation project. It is designed to support supervised field training and Rainbow Restoration procedures.

Important limitation

This is an internal company training module. It does not issue IICRC certification, replace an approved IICRC WRT course, replace the ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard, or authorize an employee to perform work outside company policy, licensing, safety, mold, asbestos, lead, electrical, confined-space, or other task-specific requirements.

Required Before Field Release

The restoration mindset

Inspect first. Make the area safe. Determine what is affected. Remove bulk water. Establish appropriate controls. Dry the structure while measuring progress. Document what you found, what you did, and why.

MODULE01

Safety & Initial Response

Make the loss safe before making it dry

1. Pause & Observe

Look for electrical hazards, slip/fall hazards, ceiling collapse, contaminated water, sharp objects, unsafe access, chemicals, animals, heat/cold stress, structural instability, and occupant vulnerabilities.

2. Verify the Source

Determine whether the water source has stopped or is being controlled. Do not assume a repaired-looking source is actually corrected.

3. Protect People

Use PPE and engineering controls appropriate to the conditions. Establish safe work zones and keep occupants away from unsafe or contaminated areas.

Initial Response Workflow

1

Site-specific safety survey

Identify hazards before work begins and reassess when conditions change.

2

Customer interview

Ask when the loss started, what happened, whether water is still entering, prior damage, repairs, and customer priorities.

3

Source and affected-area inspection

Trace visible and hidden migration instead of assuming damage stops where staining stops.

4

Photograph and document

Capture conditions before disturbance and record observations in the company systems.

Stop-work examples

Unknown contamination, sewage or grossly contaminated water beyond your assignment; strong chemical odors; suspect asbestos or lead disturbance; active electrical hazards; unstable ceilings/floors; significant visible mold; medical symptoms; or any condition you are not trained or equipped to control.

Knowledge Check

Safety, source verification, occupant concerns, utilities, and changed conditions must be addressed before production work.
2. You discover a sagging wet ceiling with active electrical fixtures below it. What should you do? Critical
Potential electrical and collapse hazards require stop-work, isolation, and qualified evaluation.
3. A technician should treat customer priorities and pre-existing damage as:
Customer concerns, contents priorities, and pre-existing conditions are part of the initial inspection and documentation.

Inspection & Preliminary Determination

Build the restoration plan from evidence, not assumptions

What the Initial Inspection Must Establish

  • Site-specific hazards and required controls
  • Water source and whether it is controlled
  • Approximate start time and duration
  • Rooms, assemblies, contents, and systems affected
  • Visible and suspected hidden migration
  • Pre-existing damage and unrelated conditions
  • Potential contamination and the preliminary water Category
  • Materials that may require specialty evaluation
  • Dry standards / reference readings and drying goals
  • Customer priorities and access constraints

Preliminary determination can change

New information can change the restoration plan. If later inspection reveals additional contamination, delayed response, hidden microbial growth, or other complications, stop and have the supervisor reassess the scope and controls.

Three Documentation Questions

What did we find?

Source, affected areas, moisture readings, contamination indicators, hazards, and pre-existing damage.

What did we decide?

Scope, drying goals, materials to restore/remove, equipment plan, containment, and specialist referrals.

What proves it?

Photos, sketches, meter readings, psychrometric data, notes, authorizations, and daily monitoring records.

Knowledge Check

1. Which item belongs in the initial inspection?
A professional inspection gathers safety, source, migration, pre-existing condition, and customer-priority information.
2. If the preliminary determination suggests contaminated Category 2 or 3 water, restorative drying should begin before contamination is addressed. Critical
The S500 framework calls for contamination controls and remediation considerations before restorative drying in contaminated Category 2/3 conditions.
3. A drying goal is best established by:
Drying goals should be tied to defensible dry standards/reference conditions and material-specific measurements.

MODULE03

Water Category & Class Basics

Two different determinations that influence scope and drying strategy

Water Category

Category is about contamination. In simplified training terms: Category 1 begins from a sanitary source; Category 2 contains significant contamination; Category 3 is grossly contaminated. The actual preliminary determination requires inspection of the source, time, affected materials, contaminants, and site conditions.

Never “upgrade” or “downgrade” a category casually. Escalate uncertain or changing conditions to a qualified supervisor.

Water Class

Class describes the approximate evaporation load created by the amount of water and the types of affected materials. Class 1 is the lightest drying load; Classes 2 and 3 represent progressively greater wetting/evaporation; Class 4 involves materials or assemblies with low-permeance or deeply bound moisture that may require specialty drying approaches.

Scenario Sort

Choose the best immediate response. These are training examples only; field determinations require site-specific inspection.

Supply line broke 20 minutes ago; clean water on sealed concrete and baseboard.
Toilet overflow includes fecal material.
Three days after a “clean” loss, wet wall cavities now show odor and suspected growth.

Knowledge Check

1. Water Category primarily describes:
Category is about contamination characteristics and exposure risk, not simply water quantity.
2. Water Class primarily helps the restorer understand:
Class is a drying/evaporation-load concept based on how much and what kind of material is wet.
3. A clean supply-line loss can always remain Category 1 regardless of time and conditions. Critical
Category can change as conditions, time, contaminants, or affected materials change. Reassess rather than relying only on the original source.

MODULE04

Moisture Mapping & Instruments

Find the true boundary of damage and record it consistently

Moisture Inspection Principles

Start broad

Use visual clues, thermal patterns when appropriate, and non-invasive scanning to identify likely affected zones.

Confirm

Use appropriate penetrating/non-penetrating instruments and methods to confirm actual material moisture.

Reference

Establish dry standards/reference conditions using comparable unaffected material when practical.

Mitigate Restoration Moisture Workflow

  1. Create or open the loss and confirm rooms/areas.
  2. Build the affected-material list and consistent reading locations.
  3. Enter initial material moisture readings and dry standards/reference readings.
  4. Enter temperature/RH and other psychrometric information required by company procedure.
  5. Update readings each monitoring visit using the same locations whenever practical.
  6. Add notes for inaccessible areas, changed conditions, removed materials, and readings that are not comparable.
  7. Do not fabricate, backfill, or estimate meter readings. If a reading was missed, document that it was not obtained.

MODULE05

Psychrometry & Drying Fundamentals

Understand the air side of structural drying

Temperature

Changes air capacity and can affect evaporation and material behavior.

Relative Humidity

Percent saturation relative to the air temperature.

GPP / Humidity Ratio

Approximate mass of water vapor in the air; useful for comparing moisture load.

Dew Point

Temperature at which air would reach saturation and condensation can occur.

The Drying System

Professional drying is a balance of water removal, evaporation, dehumidification, temperature management, and airflow. Extraction removes liquid water fastest. Air movement encourages evaporation from wet materials. Dehumidification removes water vapor from the air. Temperature influences vapor pressure and evaporation. Monitoring tells you whether the system is working.

Do not chase heat alone

More heat is not automatically better. Watch material limits, occupant safety, equipment limits, condensation risk, vapor movement into unaffected areas, and the performance of the total drying system.

MODULE06

Extraction, Cleaning & Selective Removal

Remove water and contamination efficiently while preserving restorable materials

Extraction

Remove standing and absorbed liquid water as early and thoroughly as practical. Multiple extraction passes or specialty tools may be appropriate depending on materials and access.

Cleaning / Remediation

Contaminated water losses require controls, cleaning, and removal decisions appropriate to the contamination and material. Disinfection is not a substitute for physical removal of contamination.

Selective Removal

Remove materials when they cannot be effectively restored, when required to access trapped moisture/contamination, or when the approved scope calls for removal.

Demolition hazard check

Before disturbing drywall, flooring, texture, insulation, resilient flooring, adhesives, or other suspect materials, follow Rainbow procedures for asbestos, lead, silica, electrical, and other regulated hazards. If unsure, stop and escalate.

Work Sequence Exercise

  1. 1Make site safe and establish controls
  2. 2Document pre-existing and initial conditions
  3. 3Extract bulk water where applicable
  4. 4Perform approved cleaning/removal and expose trapped wet materials as needed
  5. 5Set appropriate drying/filtration equipment
  6. 6Monitor and document drying progress

Knowledge Check

1. Which action generally removes the greatest amount of water fastest at the beginning of a wet loss?
Where applicable, aggressive extraction removes liquid water far faster than trying to evaporate it.
2. Selective demolition should be based on: Critical
Remove materials when needed for contamination removal, access, safety, or because they are not restorable—not by automatic height rules.
3. Before removing materials in an older building, technicians should consider: Critical
Disturbance can create regulated hazards. Follow company procedures before demolition.

MODULE07

Drying Equipment & Setup

Build a drying environment that is effective, safe, and monitorable

Air Movers

Promote evaporation by creating airflow at wet surfaces and within approved drying cavities. Position for coverage without creating unsafe conditions or spreading contamination.

Dehumidifiers

Remove moisture from air. Selection and quantity depend on the drying load, space conditions, equipment capability, and the restoration plan—not a single universal rule.

Air Filtration

HEPA-filtered air filtration or pressure control may be used when the project requires particulate control. It is not interchangeable with dehumidification.

Equipment Setup Checklist

Knowledge Check

1. Air movers are primarily used to:
Air movers support evaporation by moving air across wet materials and through appropriate drying zones.
2. Dehumidifiers are primarily used to:
Dehumidification lowers the air’s moisture load so evaporation can continue.
3. Electrical loading and cord placement are: Critical
Equipment placement must account for electrical capacity, GFCI/company requirements, cord management, heat, and safe egress.

MODULE08

Daily Monitoring & Adjustments

A drying plan is managed, not merely installed

Every Monitoring Visit

  • Recheck safety and changed conditions.
  • Confirm source remains controlled.
  • Verify equipment is operating and still appropriately placed.
  • Collect room/environmental psychrometric data per company procedure.
  • Collect material readings at consistent mapped locations.
  • Compare to prior readings and dry standards/goals.
  • Investigate unexpected increases or plateaus.
  • Adjust airflow/dehumidification or scope when justified and authorized.
  • Update Mitigate Restoration the same day.
  • Upload current monitoring photos/documents to Fusion.

Plateau Troubleshooting

Knowledge Check

1. Daily monitoring should include:
Monitoring is both technical and operational: materials, environment, equipment, scope, safety, and customer issues.
2. If a material is not drying as expected, the best response is: Critical
A drying plateau is information. Check source control, hidden moisture, airflow, dehumidification, vapor barriers, temperature, and scope.
3. Daily Mitigate Restoration entries should be: Critical
Moisture records should reflect actual measurements and observations from each monitoring visit.

MODULE09

Fusion Photo & Document Workflow

If the file does not show it, someone reviewing the job later may not know it happened

Photo Standard

  1. Start with exterior/address or job-identifying context when appropriate.
  2. Take wide room orientation photos.
  3. Take medium views showing the affected assembly.
  4. Take detail photos of damage, moisture evidence, source area, meter readings when useful, and pre-existing conditions.
  5. Photograph mitigation steps: extraction, removed materials, containment, cleaning, equipment, and inaccessible conditions.
  6. Take daily monitoring/progress photos when conditions change.
  7. Take completion photos after drying and equipment removal.

Fusion Upload Discipline

  • Upload photos and documents to the correct job promptly.
  • Use clear naming/notes when the image is not self-explanatory.
  • Avoid duplicates that add no value.
  • Never delete or alter documentation to hide a mistake or changed condition.
  • Do not include unnecessary sensitive personal information.
  • Make sure signed authorizations, sketches, reports, and other required records are present.
