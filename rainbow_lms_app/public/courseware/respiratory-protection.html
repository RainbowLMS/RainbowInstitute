COURSE00

Interactive Respiratory Protection Training

Course setup, prerequisites and learning path

Course Purpose

This course supports initial, annual refresher and retraining for employees who may use N95 filtering facepieces, elastomeric half-face respirators, full-face respirators or powered air-purifying respirators during restoration operations.

Authorization control

Completing this electronic module does not, by itself, authorize respirator use. Required medical clearance, fit testing for tight-fitting respirators, hands-on competency and task-specific assignment must also be current. Separate asbestos, silica, lead, HAZWOPER, confined-space, bloodborne-pathogens, chemical-specific, licensing or manufacturer training may still be required.

MODULE01

Program Overview and Respiratory Hazards

Why protection is needed, hazard recognition and the hierarchy of controls

Learning Objectives

Recognize hazards

Identify common airborne contaminants and sources encountered during restoration work.

Apply controls

Explain why elimination, engineering and work-practice controls come before respirators.

Stop unsafe work

Recognize unknown, oxygen-deficient or changing atmospheres that require reassessment.

1

Why respiratory protection is necessary

Respirators are selected only after the airborne hazard is assessed and feasible controls are considered. Wrong filters, poor fit, damaged parts, unauthorized modification or removing the respirator in a contaminated area can reduce protection and lead to inhalation exposure.

2

Common restoration hazards

Mold

Spores, fragments and dust

Disturbance of contaminated drywall, insulation and contents can aerosolize particles and microbial material.

Fire

Soot, ash and vapors

Combustion residue, char, insulation, fine dust, cleaning chemicals and odor-control products may be present.

Category 3

Bioaerosols and chemicals

Sewage and floodwater may contain bacteria, viruses, fungi, solids and chemical contaminants.

Demolition

Silica, lead and nuisance dust

Cutting, grinding, sanding and disturbing older building materials can release regulated contaminants.

Asbestos

Fibers from ACM or PACM

Suspect materials require evaluation and authorized, substance-specific procedures before disturbance.

Chemicals

VOCs, solvents and disinfectants

SDSs and labels are essential because particulate filters do not automatically protect against gases or vapors.

3

Hierarchy of controls

1

Eliminate or substitute

Avoid the hazardous process or use a less hazardous material when feasible.

2

Engineering controls

Use containment, HEPA-filtered negative air, local exhaust, wet methods and source capture.

3

Administrative and work-practice controls

Limit access, plan sequencing, minimize disturbance, use work/rest practices and provide project briefings.

4

Respiratory protection and PPE

Use a correctly selected and approved respirator as part of the complete control plan—not as a substitute for feasible controls.

Unknown or oxygen-deficient atmosphere

Do not enter with an N95, half-face APR, full-face APR or routine PAPR. Stop work, isolate the area and obtain a specialized hazard evaluation and response plan.

Module 1 Knowledge Check

1. Why is a respirator required for a task?
A respirator must be tied to a hazard assessment, feasible controls and the approved task.
2. Which control should normally be considered before relying on a respirator?
Respirators are used after feasible elimination, engineering and work-practice controls are considered.
3. What is the correct response when an unknown atmosphere is discovered?
Unknown or potentially IDLH conditions require stop-work and specialized evaluation.

MODULE02

Medical Evaluation and Fit Testing

Prerequisites, confidentiality, facial hair and seal check versus fit test

Required Sequence

1

Medical evaluation

A PLHCP determines whether the employee can safely use the respirator and identifies restrictions. Medical information remains confidential.

2

Respirator selection and fit test

Each tight-fitting make, model, style and size must be fit tested before initial use, at least annually and after fit-affecting changes.

3

Training and hands-on competency

The employee must understand and demonstrate safe use with the assigned respirator before authorization.

4

Task-specific assignment

The supervisor verifies the hazard assessment, controls, filters or cartridges, PPE, emergency plan and current records.

Medical Evaluation

  • Required before fit testing or required workplace respirator wear.
  • Questionnaire answers, diagnoses and clinical discussions are confidential.
  • Reevaluation may be needed after symptoms, PLHCP recommendation, substantial condition changes or observed difficulty.
  • Employees must report chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, dizziness, faintness, severe fatigue, confusion, claustrophobia or other difficulty.
Instructors do not diagnose employees. Stop the exercise and refer health concerns through the company medical-evaluation process.

Fit and Face-Seal Rules

  • Facial hair may not cross or interfere with the tight-fitting sealing surface or valve function.
  • Eyewear, hearing protection, hoods and other PPE must not interfere with the seal.
  • A passed fit test does not permit facial hair or substitute for a user seal check.
  • Loose-fitting PAPR alternatives may be evaluated when appropriate, but still require medical evaluation, training, selection and airflow checks.

Fit Test vs. User Seal Check

ItemFit testUser seal check
PurposeVerifies that a specific tight-fitting make, model, style and size can achieve the required fit.Checks the seal each time the respirator is donned.
FrequencyBefore initial use, at least annually and after fit-affecting changes.Every time the tight-fitting respirator is put on.
MethodQualitative or quantitative protocol conducted by a qualified person.Positive/negative pressure or manufacturer method performed by the wearer.
SubstitutionNeither substitutes for the other.

MODULE03

Respirator Types, Limitations and Selection

Equipment demonstration, filters, cartridges, SDS information and change-out rules

Selection Starts With the Hazard Assessment

Selection must consider contaminant identity and concentration, oxygen level, work duration and rate, environmental conditions, required protection factor, eye or skin irritation, compatibility with other PPE, filter or cartridge approval, service life and manufacturer instructions.

Air-purifying respirator prohibition

N95s, half-face APRs, full-face APRs and routine PAPRs do not supply oxygen. They are not used in oxygen-deficient, unknown or IDLH atmospheres unless a specific respirator and complete specialized program are approved for that condition.

N95 Filtering Facepiece Typical APF 10

Capability

Non-oil particulates where the hazard assessment, approval, fit and use support the assignment.

Critical limitations

No oxygen supply; no protection against gases or vapors; not for oil aerosols, asbestos work, unknown atmospheres or conditions beyond approval.

Elastomeric Half-Face APR Typical APF 10

Capability

Particulates or specified gases and vapors when equipped with compatible, NIOSH-approved filters or cartridges.

Critical limitations

Requires medical clearance, fit testing and a face seal; eyes are not protected; cartridge service life is limited; no oxygen supply.

Full-Face APR Typical APF 50

Capability

Particulate or specified chemical protection with eye and face coverage; may provide a higher APF when all program conditions are met.

Critical limitations

Requires fit testing and a seal; spectacle kits and other PPE must be compatible; lens condition matters; no oxygen supply.

Powered Air-Purifying Respirator Configuration dependent

Capability

Powered airflow through approved filters or cartridges. A loose-fitting hood does not depend on a tight face seal; a tight-fitting facepiece still requires fit testing and seal checks.

Critical limitations

Battery, airflow, alarms, hose, filter and headpiece must be verified. Low flow, alarm or battery failure requires immediate exit. Routine PAPRs do not make unknown or IDLH entry safe.

Atmosphere-Supplying Respirators Specialized assignment

Supplied-air respirators and SCBAs require specialized selection, breathing-air quality controls, training, emergency procedures and program elements beyond routine air-purifying respirator training. Employees are not authorized unless specifically assigned and trained.

1

Filters and cartridges

N-Series

Not resistant to oil

N95 filters are used only for approved non-oil particulate conditions.

P-Series

Strongly resistant to oil

P100 filters provide high-efficiency particulate filtration within their approved conditions. They do not protect against gases, vapors or oxygen deficiency.

OV/P100

Combination protection

Used only when both the chemical vapor and particulate hazards are within the cartridge approval and the hazard assessment supports selection.

Change-Out and Compatibility Rules

  • Use only NIOSH-approved components in the approved manufacturer configuration; do not mix brands unless expressly approved.
  • Gas and vapor cartridges require an objective change-out schedule based on manufacturer data, conditions and the written program—not odor as the planned replacement method.
  • Replace damaged, wet, contaminated, expired or difficult-to-breathe-through filters and cartridges as required.
  • Store opened chemical cartridges in an airtight container when permitted by the company schedule; label installation/open dates.
  • Review SDSs and labels for chemical identity, exposure information, controls, incompatibilities and emergency procedures.

Interactive Selection Scenarios

Select the best response. The scenario is complete after all selections are correct.

Scenario A — Mold-contaminated drywall removal

A documented assessment identifies particulate mold and dust hazards, no gas/vapor hazard and no unknown or oxygen-deficient condition.

Correct. Use the assigned respirator and P100 particulate filters supported by the project assessment and company authorization.

Scenario B — Soot cleanup with solvent-based deodorizer

The SDS and assessment identify both particulate residue and an organic-vapor exposure within an approved APR application.

Correct. Both hazard classes must be addressed, and the cartridge must be compatible, approved and managed under a change-out schedule.

Scenario C — Strong unknown odor after opening a wall cavity

The substance and concentration are not known, and the original hazard assessment did not address the condition.

Correct. Employees do not self-select cartridges for unknown atmospheres. Stop work and reassess.

MODULE04

Inspection, Donning, Doffing and User Seal Checks

Instructor demonstration, supervised practice and model-specific verification

Hands-on requirement

This screen supports instruction; the instructor must still demonstrate the actual assigned respirator and observe each employee practicing until safe performance is achieved.

Interactive Pre-Use Inspection

Check every item that must be inspected before use. When a defect is found, remove the respirator from service until properly repaired or replaced.

Put the Donning Steps in Order

Use the arrow buttons to arrange the general sequence. Always follow the manufacturer instructions for the actual respirator.

    Positive-Pressure Seal Check

    1. Cover the exhalation valve or follow the manufacturer method.
    2. Exhale gently.
    3. The facepiece should bulge slightly without air leaking around the seal.
    4. Do not perform a method that can damage the valve or respirator.

    Negative-Pressure Seal Check

    1. Cover the filter/cartridge inlets or follow the manufacturer method.
    2. Inhale gently and hold briefly.
    3. The facepiece should collapse slightly and remain collapsed without leakage.
    4. Do not enter if a satisfactory seal cannot be achieved.

    Doffing and Common Errors

    Correct approach

    • Exit the contaminated or regulated area first.
    • Follow the project decontamination sequence.
    • Handle straps rather than the contaminated facepiece where possible.
    • Move equipment to the designated cleaning or disposal area.

    Errors to correct immediately

    • Skipping the seal check or substituting it for a fit test.
    • Overtightening straps to compensate for poor fit.
    • Allowing hair, eyewear or PPE to cross the seal.
    • Removing or breaking the seal inside the contaminated area.

    Module 4 Knowledge Check

    1. A user seal check is performed:
    The wearer performs a seal check every time the tight-fitting respirator is put on.
    2. A cracked facepiece or damaged valve should be:
    Defective equipment cannot be used until properly repaired or replaced.
    3. Where may an employee remove the respirator for a break?
    Exit first, then doff in accordance with decontamination procedures.

    MODULE05

    Routine Use, Emergencies, Cleaning and Storage

    Safe work practices, immediate exit criteria, decontamination and equipment care

    Routine Use Rules

    • Use only the respirator, filters or cartridges and task for which the employee is trained and authorized.
    • Maintain the face seal; do not touch, lift or break it in the contaminated area.
    • Use established communication and buddy-awareness methods.
    • Take breaks only in a clean area after leaving and following decontamination procedures.
    • Report increased breathing resistance, damage, unexpected odor/taste/irritation, symptoms, PAPR alarm or changing conditions.
    • Account for heat stress, workload, hydration and compatible PPE.

    Immediate Exit Criteria

    • Seal is lost or facepiece becomes damaged.
    • Breathing becomes difficult or resistance increases unexpectedly.
    • Unexpected odor, taste, eye irritation or respiratory irritation occurs.
    • PAPR alarm activates, airflow drops or battery power fails.
    • Dizziness, chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, confusion, nausea or other medical symptoms occur.
    • Unknown material, uncontrolled release, oxygen-deficient or potentially IDLH condition is suspected.
    • Supervisor orders evacuation or project controls fail.

    Emergency Decision Scenarios

    Choose the first safe action in each scenario.

    Seal loss during demolition

    The facepiece shifts and a leak is felt at the cheek.

    Exit first. Do not remain in the contaminated area after a lost seal.

    PAPR low-flow alarm

    The alarm sounds while the employee is inside the work area.

    A low-flow, alarm or battery failure requires immediate exit.

    Medical symptoms

    An employee reports chest tightness, dizziness and unusual shortness of breath.

    Remove the employee from exposure and follow the medical-response process.

    Cleaning and Disinfection Sequence

    Arrange the general sequence. Always follow the manufacturer instructions and company procedure.

      Storage Practice Exercise

      Clean, dry facepiece stored without pressure on the seal
      Wet respirator sealed in a bag
      Opened organic-vapor cartridges left uncovered in the truck
      Facepiece protected from sunlight, chemicals, dust, heat and deformation

      MODULE06

      Task-Specific Hazards for Restoration Operations

      Project briefings for mold, fire, Category 3, asbestos, demolition and chemical use

      Site-specific instruction is mandatory

      Before work begins—and whenever conditions change—the supervisor must review the project hazard assessment, engineering controls, assigned respirator and filters, PPE, SDSs, decontamination, communication, emergency exit routes and stop-work triggers.

      Mold Remediation

      Hazards

      Spores, fragments, dust, contaminated insulation and possible co-hazards such as asbestos, lead or silica.

      Controls and selection

      Containment, HEPA negative air, source removal and HEPA cleaning. N95 may be limited to low-risk particulate work; half-face P100 or higher may be required for demolition based on assessment.

      Stop-work triggers

      Containment failure, loss of negative pressure, unexpected material, uncontrolled dust or conditions beyond the assessment.

      Fire and Smoke Restoration

      Hazards

      Soot, ash, char, fine particulates, fiberglass, insulation, VOCs, cleaning agents and odor-control chemicals.

      Controls and selection

      HEPA vacuuming, negative air, local exhaust and wet methods. P100 addresses particulates; OV/P100 may be needed when the SDS and assessment identify organic vapor plus particulate hazards.

      Stop-work triggers

      Unexpected chemical odor or irritation, damaged structure, unidentified residue, control failure or new regulated material.

      Category 3 Water and Biohazards

      Hazards

      Bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, sewage solids, bioaerosols and chemical contaminants.

      Controls and selection

      Containment, controlled demolition, splash reduction, HEPA negative air where appropriate and P100 or OV/P100 based on both biological and chemical hazards.

      Stop-work triggers

      Needlestick/sharps hazard, unknown chemical, uncontrolled aerosol generation, medical symptoms or containment failure.

      Asbestos Awareness and Work Restrictions

      Do not disturb suspect material without authorization.

      Asbestos work must follow the applicable work class, exposure assessment, regulated-area, competent-person, medical surveillance, fit testing, training, licensing and project requirements. This respiratory course does not replace asbestos-specific training or authorization.

      Controls

      Critical barriers, negative-pressure enclosures, HEPA exhaust, wet methods, HEPA vacuuming, regulated areas and decontamination.

      Stop-work triggers

      Suspect ACM/PACM, containment breach, uncontrolled fiber release, respirator failure or work outside authorization.

      Silica, Lead and Demolition Hazards

      Hazards

      Respirable crystalline silica, lead dust, drywall dust, wood dust, insulation fibers and unknown building materials.

      Controls and selection

      Pre-assessment, regulated-material evaluation, wet methods, HEPA local capture, restricted access and exposure-based respirator selection.

      Stop-work triggers

      Unknown material, visible dust beyond the control plan, tool/control failure or scope change.

      Chemicals, Disinfectants and Odor Control

      Hazards

      VOCs, solvents, disinfectant mist, fogging chemicals, coatings, adhesives, encapsulants and incompatible mixtures.

      Controls and selection

      Review SDS and labels; use substitution, ventilation, isolation and application controls. Select only approved cartridges with a change-out schedule when required.

      Stop-work triggers

      Unexpected odor or irritation, incompatible product, labeling restriction, ventilation failure, spill or unknown concentration.

      Project Briefing Checklist

      • Identified and potential respiratory hazards
      • Engineering and work-practice controls
      • Assigned respirator make, model, style and size
      • Filters/cartridges and change-out schedule
      • Applicable SDSs, labels and exposure information
      • Other PPE and compatibility
      • Communication, exit, accountability and reentry
      • Decontamination, cleaning, waste and changed conditions

      MODULE07

      Knowledge and Practical Evaluation

      Assessment, coaching, hands-on competency, acknowledgment and authorization

      Knowledge Standard

      The company performance standard is at least 80 percent plus correct understanding of all critical life-safety concepts. The electronic assessment may be retried after coaching.

      Practical Standard

      All critical hands-on steps must be performed safely with the assigned equipment. Attendance or a passing quiz alone does not establish competency.

      Authorization

      The Program Administrator or designee verifies medical, fit-test, training, practical evaluation and task assignment records before respirator-required work.

      Critical Errors

      • Using the wrong respirator, filter or cartridge.
      • Entering after a failed seal check.
      • Breaking the seal in a contaminated area without exiting.
      • Failing to recognize an emergency exit condition.
      • Continuing after a PAPR alarm or airflow failure.
      • Using equipment outside its approval.
      • Cleaning or storing equipment in a way that makes it unsafe.
      • Continuing after changed or unknown conditions.
