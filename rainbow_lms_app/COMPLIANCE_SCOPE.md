# Compliance Scope and Assignment Guidance

**Catalog review date: August 31, 2026**

This LMS includes a broad restoration-industry safety and compliance catalog, but no static catalog can determine every requirement for every employee. Course assignment must be based on actual duties, foreseeable hazards, exposures, equipment, written programs, competent-person decisions, and the jurisdiction where work occurs.

## Core assignment principle

Administrators should use this sequence:

1. Identify the employee’s tasks and work environment.
2. Identify applicable OSHA/VOSH standards and company written programs.
3. Determine whether knowledge training, hands-on demonstration, qualified-person instruction, medical clearance, fit testing, exposure assessment, licensing, or external certification is required.
4. Assign the appropriate LMS course and practical task.
5. Record evaluation and task-specific authorization.
6. Retrain when required by the standard, program, observed performance, changed conditions, new equipment, or expiration policy.

## Important course limits

### Asbestos

Internal LMS completion does not replace any required EPA-accredited course, Virginia asbestos license, project notification, survey, competent-person supervision, medical surveillance, fit testing, air monitoring, regulated-area controls, or waste documentation.

### Respiratory protection

A course certificate does not authorize respirator use. Authorization may require a written program, hazard evaluation, medical clearance before required use/fit testing, fit testing for the specific respirator, equipment assignment, cartridge/change-out decisions, hands-on competency, and task-specific approval.

### Lead and silica

Awareness or knowledge training does not replace exposure assessment, regulated-area controls, competent-person duties, respiratory/PPE prerequisites, medical surveillance when triggered, or other substance-specific requirements.

### Bloodborne pathogens

Assign based on occupational exposure. Employees with occupational exposure may require an exposure-control plan, engineering/work-practice controls, vaccination offer, post-exposure procedures, and annual training beyond a generic awareness course.

### Forklifts and aerial lifts

LMS knowledge training does not by itself establish operator qualification. Operator training/evaluation must include equipment- and workplace-specific practical components where required.

### First aid and CPR

Awareness content is not a first-aid or CPR certification course. Use an approved hands-on provider when certification is required by company policy, customer requirement, or the applicable standard.

### HAZWOPER

The HAZWOPER course is intentionally framed as awareness and emergency stop-work/escalation. It does not grant 24-hour or 40-hour HAZWOPER qualification or authorize uncontrolled hazardous-substance response.

### Heat illness

The LMS includes a heat-illness prevention course and company-control framework. As of the review date, federal OSHA continued rulemaking for a specific heat standard, while existing requirements and recognized hazard controls still applied. Virginia-specific requirements should be reviewed as the state develops and adopts its standard.

## Restoration technical standards

Water, fire, mold, and contents modules are internal company training informed by restoration-industry principles and the supplied course structure. Completion does not issue IICRC certification. External certification requires the applicable approved training/testing process.
